How the South African review works

Two passes, a verification layer written in plain code, then South African law applied on top. The findings that matter never rest on the model doing the sum correctly, or on it remembering a section number.

Step 1 of 3 · Extract

The document and its attachments are read, and the facts are written down as structured data: amounts, payment dates, parties, quantities. Nothing is judged at this stage.

Step 2 of 3 · Verify

The numbers are worked out again in plain code. Does the payment schedule reconcile to the total? Does a South African ID number pass its checksum? Was the company registered at CIPC before the date it supposedly signed?

Step 3 of 3 · Apply SA law

Only the statutes your document actually engages are applied. The Rental Housing Act for a lease, the Alienation of Land Act for a sale of property. Every citation is then checked against a curated list before you see it.

What South African documents it reviews

Property

Offers to purchase, deeds of sale, lease and rental agreements. Checks the Alienation of Land Act writing and written-authority requirements, and the Property Practitioners Act Fidelity Fund Certificate and disclosure form.

Commercial

Supplier and service agreements, NDAs, sale of business, shareholder agreements. Checks Companies Act authority, business rescue exposure and penalty clauses.

Credit and security

Loan agreements and suretyships. Checks National Credit Act rate caps, both limbs of the in duplum rule, and the writing and signature requirements for a surety.

Employment

Contracts of employment and restraints of trade, against the LRA, the BCEA earnings threshold and the national minimum wage.

Consumer

Any transaction the Consumer Protection Act touches, including unfair terms, exemption clauses that were never drawn to your attention, and voetstoots.

Deal bundles

Up to five documents reviewed together, so an exhibit that contradicts the covering letter is caught rather than read in isolation.

Why the findings hold up

Built to be checked, not trusted

  • The risk score comes from arithmetic and calendar checks written in plain code, so the same document always scores the same.
  • Citations are limited to a curated list of South African statutes and judgments, and every one is checked before it reaches you. South African courts have ordered costs against practitioners personally for filing AI-fabricated authorities, so this is not a nicety.
  • Personal identifiers are replaced with placeholders before any text is sent for analysis, and restored only in the report on your screen.
  • Where a statutory threshold is uncertain, the report says so instead of stating it as settled.

Common questions about contract review in South Africa

Does Legal-Eye work for South African contracts?

Yes. It is built for South African law specifically, not adapted to it. Reviews apply the Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008, the National Credit Act 34 of 2005, the Alienation of Land Act 68 of 1981, the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002, the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and the Rental Housing Act 50 of 1999, among others, together with the leading judgments on contract such as Beadica 231 CC v Trustees Oregon Trust and Barkhuizen v Napier. Amounts are read in Rand.

Is this legal advice?

No. Legal-Eye is an automated triage tool, not a law firm, and using it creates no attorney and client relationship. Under the Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014, only an admitted legal practitioner may appear in court or prepare documents for use in legal proceedings. Legal-Eye does neither, and it should not be used for court papers. Use it to work out what needs an attorney's attention, then take it to one.

What does a contract review cost in South Africa?

An attorney reviewing the same agreement properly will usually charge several thousand rand, and for a document that matters that is money well spent. Legal-Eye is for working out which documents are worth that spend.

Can it invent a section number or a case?

This is the risk that matters most, and it is handled directly. South African courts have ordered costs against practitioners personally and referred them to the Legal Practice Council for filing AI-fabricated authorities. Mavundla v MEC: COGTA KZN and Northbound Processing v SADPMR are the cautionary cases. Legal-Eye may cite only from a curated, human-checked list, and every citation is measured against that list before you see it. Anything outside it is flagged as unverified rather than published as fact.

Is my document stored anywhere, and is it POPIA compliant?

Your file is read in memory, written to a temporary location only while being analysed, and deleted immediately afterwards. Before any text is sent for analysis, personal identifiers are replaced with placeholders. That covers South African ID numbers, email addresses, telephone numbers and account numbers. They are restored only in the report on your screen. Analysis is performed by a third-party AI provider outside South Africa. That is disclosed here, as section 18 of POPIA requires, and set out in full in our privacy notice.

Which file formats are supported?

PDF, DOCX and TXT, including text inside DOCX tables. Scanned PDFs with no text layer are read with OCR, which matters here, since most signed South African agreements arrive as scans.

Can it get things wrong?

Yes. Any AI tool can misread a document, and OCR can misread names, figures and dates. That is why the verification pass redoes the arithmetic in plain code rather than trusting the model, why citations are restricted to a curated list, and why every report ends with a disclaimer. Check anything important against the original.
